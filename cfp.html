From reasoning to a pilot decision

What evidence would justify or rule out a bounded, accountable pilot for an AI agent?

Reasoning to Readiness brings together researchers, industrial developers, operations teams, assurance practitioners, and students to investigate how explicit knowledge, rules, executable constraints, and causal models change what an agent can reliably do. We welcome results from simulations, prototypes, pilots, and production, as well as work in progress, negative results, and testable positions.

Contributions will inform a shared deployment evidence contract that connects a system’s intended use and claims to tests, supporting artifacts, unresolved risks, and the person accountable for the decision.

AAAI approval is pending. Paper submissions will open only if the symposium is accepted. Expressions of interest are welcome now.

Topics of interest

Choose the area that best fits your question. Cross-area contributions are welcome.

01

Cognitive architectures and adaptive memory

Working and persistent memory, reusable procedures and skills, inspectable planning, knowledge provenance and revision, and transfer across tasks. Evaluate what happens when a stored fact or learned procedure becomes stale.

02

Neuro-symbolic grounding and verifiable action

Knowledge graphs and ontologies, neural-to-symbolic translation, executable rules, constrained planning, independent verifiers, and tool authorization. Specify what is checked, the assumptions behind the check, and how the system handles contradictions or violations.

03

Causal and counterfactual decision support

Structural causal models, interventions, counterfactuals, identification under incomplete evidence, and calibrated abstention. Distinguish predictive performance from evidence about an intervention. Report assumptions and say when the agent should defer.

04

Industry readiness and accountable operation

Integration, policy compliance, human approvals, adversarial testing, observability, recovery after partial execution, and cost or operator effort. Report the operating conditions, trade-offs, and evidence needed to decide whether to proceed.

Contribution formats

These lengths are proposed. Benchmarks and evaluation artifacts can accompany research papers, cases, or demonstrations.

Research and position papers and posters
Submit a long paper (up to 4 pages), a short paper (up to 2 pages), or a poster abstract (up to 1 page). A limited number of posters may be accepted without a presentation slot.
Present a method, evaluation, benchmark, or testable position. Include available results or a concrete plan that could challenge the claim. Negative results and early-stage work are welcome.
Industry and operational case studies Up to 4 pages
Describe an operational decision, current workflow or baseline, observed evidence, and a failure or limitation. State whether the work is synthetic, laboratory, pilot, or production. Cases are open to teams from any affiliation.
Reproducible demonstrations Up to 2 pages
Provide a technical description, run instructions for a working or replayable artifact, and a scenario showing how the system handles a failure, invalid action, or missing evidence.
Participation statements Up to 1 page
Describe the perspective or problem you would bring to a working group and a question you would like to investigate with other participants. Students and practitioners are encouraged to contribute.

What to include

For papers, cases, and demos, attach or summarize a compact evidence contract. For early work, identify the evidence still needed and how you would obtain it.

  1. Workflow and claim. State the decision, permitted actions, operating boundaries, and one claim a reviewer can test.
  2. Architecture and comparison. Identify the learned components and the knowledge, rules, verifier, or causal model that changes behavior. Where applicable, compare against the same workflow with that component disabled or against a credible simpler baseline with comparable information and resources.
  3. Evidence and limits. Describe test conditions and available artifacts. Report uncertainty, a failure or untested condition, and the expected fallback. Where relevant, report latency, cost, and human effort alongside task success.
  4. Decision and accountability. Explain what the evidence would support, which risks remain, and who would authorize a pilot, intervene, or stop the system.

Example · Synthetic supplier-delay and approval workflow

Compare the same agent with and without an executable approval check. Test a stale supplier update and a missing approval separately and together. Measure unauthorized actions, appropriate and unnecessary escalations, and latency. Record which decisions still require the operations owner.

Share only material you have permission to release. When original data or code is restricted, describe the restriction and offer a sanitized trace, synthetic replay, or test specification where feasible.

Review and working sessions

Proposed criteria are fit to the symposium, clarity and testability of the claim, quality and limits of the evidence appropriate to the contribution, and value for discussion. Papers and cases are planned to receive at least two reviews from reviewers without conflicts. Positions and work in progress will be assessed on the clarity of the question and the proposed test.

Posters will be assessed using the same criteria, with evidence expectations appropriate to the format. Poster acceptance and presentation selection are separate decisions. A limited number of posters may be accepted without a presentation slot.

Selected presentations and case studies will feed two working labs. Participants will develop tests for a synthetic supplier-delay and approval workflow, then stress-test recovery and apply the contract to a second service-operations scenario. The proposed outputs are a revised evidence contract, a failure-and-recovery taxonomy, a cross-domain case matrix, and a research roadmap.
